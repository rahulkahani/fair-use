# Showcase plan

## Positioning

**Fair Use: copyright education and review preparation for creators.**

Public description:

> A free, open skill to explore copyright concepts, organize your source material and permissions, and prepare informed questions for a qualified lawyer. This interactive demo uses fictional examples to show the workflow. It does not determine whether an image is legal to use.

Make education, evidence gathering, and professional-review preparation the benefit. Avoid “AI lawyer,” “copyright detector,” “legal approval,” “safe to publish,” accuracy claims that have not been established, and promises that the skill replaces or reduces the need for a lawyer.

## A 60–90 second recording

| Time | Show | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10s | The opening headline and educational-demo notice | “You’ve remixed an artwork. What should you understand before deciding what to do with it? I built an open skill to help creators gather the facts and prepare better copyright questions.” |
| 10–25s | The schematic source and Photoshop remix, then **Sell it as a poster** | “This is a fictional example. Assume someone keeps a distinctive character, changes the colors and background, and wants to sell prints. This walkthrough is prewritten; no AI analysis is running here.” |
| 25–40s | Expand purpose, amount, and market considerations | “The workflow looks beyond how different the image appears. What was borrowed? Why is it needed? What’s the intended use? What information is missing?” |
| 40–55s | Switch to **Discuss it in a critical essay**, then **Obtain a suitable license** | “A different use can raise different questions. Permission is also a separate basis to investigate. None of these buttons is legal approval.” |
| 55–75s | **Prepare your own brief**; enter fictional project details and download | “You can organize your sources, permission evidence, intended use, and questions into a brief. Images stay local in this demo and are not analyzed. The brief can support a conversation with a lawyer.” |
| 75–90s | The GitHub link and disclaimer | “The skill and demo are open source. This is an educational aid, not legal advice or a substitute for professional review.” |

Keep the fictional-example and no-live-AI labels visible in the recording. Use only your own, suitably licensed, or deliberately schematic visuals; a real famous artist’s name and work are not necessary to demonstrate the workflow. Do not present a fabricated scenario as an actual dispute or client result.

## Show the actual skill in a separate segment

To demonstrate real model behavior, switch to a compatible assistant where the skill is installed or its complete chat instructions are attached. Say that you are now leaving the authored web demo. Use a fictional brief or material you are authorized to share, attach the relevant images, and ask:

> Use the fair-use skill to explain the copyright considerations in this project and prepare questions for a qualified lawyer. Identify what you inspected, what is assumed, what is unknown, and which current sources you verified. Do not provide legal clearance.

Record the actual response, including its questions and limitations. Disclose missing browsing or image capabilities. If you edit the recording for length, label cuts; do not substitute the website’s prewritten output for an actual model response or imply the website ran the skill. A successful demonstration is not evidence of a validated legal accuracy rate.

>
> This video shows a fictional, prewritten web demo—not a legal verdict or live AI analysis. The demo also lets you prepare your own project brief locally.

>
> Explore the source and download the skill: https://github.com/rahulkahani/fair-use

## Other content formats

- A carousel showing one fictional source in three different usage contexts, ending with the questions that remain.
- A screen recording that follows a permission document from “unknown” to a list of scope and authority checks, without declaring it legally sufficient.
- A short tutorial assembling a source-and-permissions brief for a fictional client project.
- A conversation with a qualified copyright lawyer reviewing representative outputs, with their actual feedback and limits accurately described.
