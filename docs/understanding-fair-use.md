# Understanding fair use before asking an AI

Prepared September 7, 2026. This is a general US-oriented introduction, not a legal opinion about your work. Other countries use different exceptions.

The useful starting question is: **what gives me a basis to make this particular use of this particular material?** Fair use is one possible answer. Ownership, permission, a suitable license, public-domain status, or the absence of borrowed protected expression may answer the question first.

## Fair use is contextual

US law asks about the use's purpose and character, the nature of the original, how much and what significant material was used, and the effect on relevant markets. These interact; a score of “three out of four” is not a legal test. [17 USC §107](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#107)

Criticism, comment, teaching and research can support an argument, but labels alone do not settle it. Noncommercial work is not automatically fair, and commercial work is not automatically excluded. Reproducing an entire image is not always fatal; copying a small but important portion is not automatically safe. [US Copyright Office overview](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/more-info.html)

The Supreme Court's [Warhol decision](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/21-869_87ad.pdf) illustrates why a striking visual change is not enough by itself. The case's Supreme Court holding concerned the first factor for a specific commercial licensing use; it was not a blanket judgment about every artistic remix. That limited holding is why this skill asks what you actually plan to do with the work.

## Practical distinctions

The following are fictional starting points for assessment, not determinations:

| Situation | First issue to examine |
|---|---|
| A Canva asset used in a design under a suitable license | Whether the actual license covers the asset and use; fair use may be unnecessary |
| An image reproduced in a detailed critique of that image | Whether the borrowing serves the actual commentary and is proportionate to it |
| An AI-restyled image sold as decorative merchandise | Whether retained expression is being used for a similar commercial purpose |
| An independently drawn image using a general artistic technique | Whether any specific protected expression was borrowed at all |
| A photo found on Pinterest with no owner or license information | Provenance and permission are unresolved; discovery online is not permission |
| A composition combining licensed and unlicensed elements | Each material component needs its own basis |
| A fully generated output with no supplied reference | Available evidence can be assessed, but universal originality cannot be certified |
| A public-domain artwork with later additions | Which version and contributions are used, and the relevant country's status |

There is no “change 30%” rule. Adding a credit or changing the medium does not supply permission. [Copyright Office FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html)

## AI and Canva do not collapse the questions

An output may contain human contributions eligible for protection while still raising a concern about someone else's work. Conversely, an output's lack of copyright protection does not erase rights in material it contains. US Copyright Office guidance distinguishes sufficient human expression from prompts alone when discussing authorship. [Part 2 announcement](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html)

For Canva, check the asset-specific license and any relevant AI terms. A library image incorporated into an output remains subject to its licensed-content rules. Provider statements about ownership between you and the provider do not establish that every third-party claim has been cleared. [Canva AI Product Terms](https://www.canva.com/policies/ai-product-terms/)

## What to bring to an assessment

Bring the finished work; relevant source images if any; where and how it will be used; what was copied or uploaded; permission/license evidence; and known disputes. Specify the relevant country or countries. If something is unknown, say so. It is better to receive a useful conditional answer than a confident answer built on invented facts.

An AI can organize evidence, explain arguments, find relevant authorities, and help identify alternatives. It can also miss visual details, misunderstand law, or invent a citation. Inspect the cited sources and use a qualified local lawyer when the consequences or uncertainty justify one. The skill is designed to expose these limits rather than hide them in a badge or percentage.
