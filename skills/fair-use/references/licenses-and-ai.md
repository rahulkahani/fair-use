# Licenses, AI and separate rights

Reviewed 2026-09-07. Retrieve the actual applicable terms; a provider's name or subscription badge is not evidence of a specific grant.

## License review

Record the asset identifier, creator/licensor, provenance, actual license text or link, version/date, acquisition/export date when relevant, and proposed use. Examine attribution, changes, commercial use, standalone resale, templates, sublicensing or client transfer, geographic restrictions, and relevant account-specific conditions. Unknown terms mean unknown permission, not automatically fair use or infringement.

For Creative Commons, identify the exact license and version. [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) allows sharing and adaptations, including commercial uses, subject to its conditions such as appropriate credit, a license link and change indications. Other CC licenses differ. The deed is a summary; follow the legal code when deciding a contested issue. CC also cautions that other rights may remain. A random uploader's CC label does not prove that uploader can license every incorporated element.

## Canva

Separate Canva library elements, templates, user uploads, and generated material. Consult the [Content License Agreement](https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/) for the asset and use, and the [AI Product Terms](https://www.canva.com/policies/ai-product-terms/) for relevant AI activity. Distinguish current terms from archived versions that applied to an earlier acquisition.

For older work, use Canva's [policy archives](https://www.canva.com/policies/policy-archives/), the user's export/acquisition record and applicable agreement to identify a plausible version. Record the exact version URL and date displayed; an archive label alone does not prove applicability. If dates or contractual applicability remain unclear, analyze supplied terms conditionally and mark license permission unresolved rather than substituting today's terms retroactively.

For template sales, ask whether buyers receive a Canva-only template or an externally distributed editable file, and identify Free/Pro or other asset categories. As checked on 2026-09-07, the agreement distinguishes Canva-only design templates in §5 from additional template-distribution permissions for Free Content in §6, which are unavailable to a design containing Pro Content. Treat this as a dated research lead: recheck all relevant restrictions and exact asset terms before applying it.

Canva's AI terms allocate responsibility for inputs and outputs to the user and distinguish ownership language between Canva and the user from rights in incorporated licensed content. For example, modifying a library photo does not necessarily give the user ownership of that photo. Do not infer that Canva Pro, an export, an AI edit, or a template makes every proposed reuse permissible. Review the exact grant and restrictions rather than memorizing product-specific safe lists.

## AI authorship and infringement are separate questions

The US Copyright Office's [2025 Part 2 announcement](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html) explains that copyrightability depends on sufficient human-determined expression; prompts alone do not establish authorship, while qualifying human arrangements or modifications may be protected. AI assistance does not categorically disqualify a larger human-authored work. Refer to the [report and current updates](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/) when analyzing an actual authorship question.

This addresses whether human contributions may receive protection. It does not establish whether an output reproduces someone else's protected expression. Do not infer that an output lacking its own copyright is automatically free of underlying third-party rights. Nor does a vendor's allocation of output rights guarantee copyright subsists or that no third party can assert rights.

Investigate meaningful human choices, source images, image-to-image inputs, traces, retained details, and the proposed output use. Do not require disclosure of the model's entire training set or speculate that a training-data dispute decides a particular output. A text prompt naming a style is not by itself proof of copied expression; an output still needs fact-specific inspection.

## Related issues

Where the actual artifact raises them, distinguish trademark or misleading endorsement, a person's likeness/privacy, contractual restrictions, moral rights, and material such as embedded photographs or logos. These can require separate review. Do not imply that the word “fair use” disposes of them, and do not generate an unrelated legal checklist for a simple copyright question.
