# Jurisdiction routing

Reviewed 2026-09-07. Detailed US coverage; non-US pointers only. This file is not a comparative legal opinion or an exhaustive list of local exceptions.

Ask which countries and uses are relevant. Do not choose the most favorable law or assume the uploader's residence alone decides a cross-border publication. If jurisdiction is unresolved, explain alternatives conditionally and identify the need for local advice where consequential.

For cross-border use, list the relevant acts (creation/input upload, distribution, sale, advertising), where they occur if known, and targeted markets. Separate identified local uses; do not attempt a definitive choice-of-law ruling from those facts alone. A requested US-only hypothetical can still be explained, but label it as such and leave other local legality unresolved. If the user needs advice about exposure across countries, identify that precise question for local counsel rather than applying one convenient exception everywhere.

## Canada

Canada uses fair dealing with statutory purposes. [Copyright Act s29](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/Section-29.html) names research, private study, education, parody and satire. Also investigate the separate criticism/review and news-reporting provisions in ss29.1–29.2, including their conditions. A qualifying purpose and fairness are distinct questions; do not replace Canadian analysis with a US factor scorecard. Retrieve applicable Canadian authorities before applying the fairness test.

Start with [CCH Canadian Ltd. v. Law Society of Upper Canada, 2004 SCC 13](https://decisions.scc-csc.ca/scc-csc/scc-csc/en/2125/1/document.do), especially paragraphs 48–60: purpose, character, amount, alternatives, nature and effect are relevant fairness considerations. These are contextual, non-exhaustive considerations, not six automatic passes. CCH is a starting authority; research subsequent relevant Canadian decisions and current statutory purposes for the actual task.

The separate [non-commercial user-generated-content provision, s29.21](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/section-29.21.html), has multiple conditions, including solely noncommercial use, reasonable attribution, a reasonable belief about lawful source material, and no substantial adverse effect on exploitation or a relevant market. It concerns a new work in which copyright subsists. Do not assume every AI remix meets that requirement or that every noncommercial remix qualifies. Read the complete current provision before applying it.

## United Kingdom

Find the particular exception and its conditions. The [UK Intellectual Property Office guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/exceptions-to-copyright) describes fair dealing for criticism, review and quotation, and parody/caricature/pastiche. The current-events reporting exception excludes photographs; this is not a blanket exclusion of photographs from all exceptions. Verify the relevant current legislation and attribution requirements. Do not assume US fair use covers a UK use.

## Other jurisdictions and international distribution

Research the specific local statute, official guidance and relevant decisions before a tailored conclusion. Do not claim a single universal “EU fair use” test. If authoritative coverage cannot be obtained, use “jurisdiction-specific analysis needed” and provide source/permission questions and practical alternatives. Explain that a source's public-domain or rights status can differ by territory.
